# Accessibility statement

Last updated Oct 12, 2026 (version 0.1.29).

Dawn Patrol is meant to be usable by everyone who surfs, including people who are blind or have low vision, are color blind, have dyslexia, find small targets or animation hard, or use only a keyboard. This page says what the site does, and where it falls short.

## What you can change

Open **Menu**, then **Accessibility**.

- **Text size**: normal, large or extra large.
- **Contrast**: follows your device, or force normal or high.
- **Text spacing**: wider letters, lines and words, plus extra space between paragraphs.
- **Color vision**: color-blind-safe score colors (a palette chosen to stay distinct for red-green and blue-yellow color blindness), and each score level also gets a pattern, so color is never the only way to tell them apart.
- **Font**: an easier-to-read typeface (Atkinson Hyperlegible, from the Braille Institute). It is downloaded from Google Fonts only when you choose it.
- **Animation**: follows your device's reduce-motion setting, or turn all animation off.
- **Read aloud speed**: slower, normal, faster or fastest.

These settings are stored on your device and are included when you export your data.

## Read aloud

The **Read aloud** button on a spot's Now tab speaks the conditions right now, rip current risk and weather alerts, a caution for beginners when the surf could be big, and the next few days. Pause and Stop are next to it.

It is built straight from the numbers on screen. No AI writes it, so it cannot make up a figure. Your device's own voice speaks it. Browsers that only offer a cloud voice may send the spoken text to the voice provider; the site prefers an on-device voice when one exists. It is English only for now.

## Hearing a chart

Every chart has a **♪ Hear it** button and the tide curve has **♪ Hear the tide curve**. The values play as a pitch that rises and falls with the line, with a higher ping marking right now. Your screen reader is told the lowest and highest values. Charts also read out a summary of range and peak, and you can move along them with the arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End.

## Keyboard and screen readers

- A **Skip to the forecast** link appears on your first Tab press.
- Every section and chart has a **Source** button, a real button you can reach with Tab, that opens a menu of links to the data behind it. Right-click and press-and-hold open the same menu for people who use them. The menu works with the arrow keys, Home, End and Escape, and Escape returns focus to the button.
- Dialogs keep focus inside them and close with Escape.
- The Read aloud and sound buttons are at least 44 by 44 pixels on touch screens.
- The site follows your device's contrast and reduce-motion settings.

## Known limits

We would rather tell you than overstate it.

- **Not formally audited.** We have not had an outside accessibility audit and we do not claim WCAG conformance. The design aims at WCAG 2.2 AA.
- **Limited screen reader testing.** The code follows ARIA patterns, and checks were done with automated browser tests and manual keyboard use, but not with real NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver or TalkBack sessions yet.
- **The map** is visual. Everything on it is also in the lists beside it, so you can pick a spot without the map.
- **Translations.** The newest labels (Read aloud, Hear it and the new settings) are in English only. Read aloud speaks English only.
- **Source menu on real devices.** Press and hold was tested with simulated touch, not on real phones, and the Menu key and Shift+F10 were not tested here, so use the Source button if they do not work for you. Source links open pages on other sites that we do not control, and their own accessibility varies.
- **Third-party content.** Map tiles, weather alerts and some tide text come from outside sources and are shown as they arrive.

## Tell us

If something does not work for you, open **Menu**, then **Report a problem**. It saves a short log on your device that you can send by email yourself. Say what you were trying to do and which device or assistive tool you use, and leave out sensitive personal information. We will try to fix real barriers quickly.
